Residency

How to assess a second residency

The best residency is not the one with the fastest approval or lowest headline cost. It is the one that actually supports the life, business and future you are trying to build.

Private client briefingApprox. 7 minute read

Most people compare residency programmes by looking at the headline numbers. We start somewhere else.

Start with the outcome

How much does it cost? How quickly can it be obtained? How little time do I need to spend there? Those questions matter, but they are rarely where we start.

A residency permit is not just a document. It creates an ongoing legal relationship with a jurisdiction, and the value of that relationship depends on what you actually need it to do.

For one client, the priority might be mobility. For another, it might be a credible route to permanent residence. Someone else may need a jurisdiction that works with their business structure, family circumstances or wider tax position.

If somebody wants a genuine fallback jurisdiction, that immediately raises different questions from somebody who simply wants additional travel flexibility. If the goal is eventual citizenship, then a low-maintenance temporary residency may be less useful than a programme with more demanding physical-presence requirements but a credible route to permanence.

The principle

The programme has to fit the strategy, not the other way around.

What does the residency actually require?

Some programmes are relatively light-touch once approved. Others require meaningful physical presence, continued investment, accommodation, insurance, income thresholds or periodic filings.

These requirements need to be looked at in practice, not just on paper. A condition that appears manageable during the application may become inconvenient once you are dividing your time between multiple countries, running a business elsewhere or dealing with changes in family circumstances.

We pay particular attention to what happens after approval, because that is where many otherwise attractive programmes become less useful.

What happens at renewal?

The initial application is only the first stage. Renewal may involve proving that the original qualifying conditions are still being met. Rules can also change over time, particularly around investment thresholds, physical presence and progression to permanent status.

Experience matters here because not every issue is solved by reading the current programme brochure. You need to understand how the rules are being applied locally, what authorities are actually asking applicants to produce and where there is legitimate flexibility in satisfying those requirements.

That is why we work with people on the ground in the jurisdictions we use.

How does it interact with everything else?

Residency is only one part of the client's wider position. A permit may affect where a client spends time, where they bank, how they structure a business, where their family is based and how their tax residence needs to be analysed.

Those issues are connected even though the underlying legal rules are not always the same.

This is also where sequencing becomes important. Sometimes the best residency application is not the first thing that should happen. A banking relationship may need to be established first. A corporate structure may need to be adjusted. Another jurisdiction may provide a cleaner route toward the same objective.

We are not interested in forcing a client through the most obvious programme simply because it is familiar.

Using the full scope of the rules

The difference between an average outcome and a very good one is often understanding how much room actually exists inside the legal framework.

That might mean choosing a less obvious qualifying route, structuring an investment differently, changing the order in which applications are made or using a jurisdiction that another adviser would not have considered because it requires more work.

Everything still has to stand up legally. But lawful does not have to mean conventional.

Our approach

Our role is to understand the boundaries properly and use the flexibility that genuinely exists within them.

The practical takeaway

There is no universally best second residency. There is only the residency that best serves the wider objective.

That is why we begin with where the client wants to end up, then build the route backwards — looking at presence, renewal, family rights, tax interaction, permanence and the practical reality of maintaining the status over time.

The finished solution should feel simple. Getting it there usually is not.
