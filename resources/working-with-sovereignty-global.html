Working with Sovereignty Global

International planning rarely involves one problem in one country. Our role is to make the different jurisdictions, advisers and institutions work as one strategy.

How we workApprox. 7 minute read

A client may need residency in one jurisdiction, banking in another, a business structure somewhere else and tax advice covering all three. The difficult part is making sure they work together.

1. Discovery

The first stage is understanding what the client actually wants.

Someone asks for a particular residency because they have heard about it from a friend or seen it marketed online. Another client thinks they need an offshore company when the real issue is banking. Somebody else wants to change tax residence but has not considered what that means for their family or business.

We do not assume the requested product is the real objective.

We ask what the client wants their life, wealth and business to look like once the work is finished. That gives us the destination.

2. Strategy

Once the destination is clear, we build backwards.

Which jurisdiction should come first? Does the client need residency before banking, or would establishing a financial relationship earlier make the later application stronger? Should a corporate structure be changed before the client moves? Are there timing issues that affect tax residence? Would a less obvious jurisdiction provide a better overall result?

This is where experience matters most. There is usually more than one lawful route available. The obvious route is not always the best one.

Our role

Understand the limits properly, identify where legitimate flexibility exists, then build the sequence that gives the client the strongest practical outcome.

3. Working inside the rules — not merely following the default route

We are comfortable dealing with situations that do not fit neatly into an off-the-shelf package.

International law, banking and immigration systems are full of thresholds, exceptions, alternative routes, discretionary criteria and different ways of satisfying the same underlying requirement.

Using those routes is not about hiding anything. It is about knowing the rules well enough to understand what they actually permit.

A conventional adviser may stop when the standard route does not fit. We look at what else is available.

If there is a lawful way to reach the client's objective that requires more coordination, more local knowledge or more creative sequencing, that is the work we are prepared to do.

4. Execution

A strategy is only useful if it can be implemented.

Once the structure is agreed, we coordinate the practical work. Depending on the engagement, that can include residency applications, documentation, banking introductions, legal and tax advisers, corporate matters and communication between different professionals in different jurisdictions.

The client should not have to act as project manager between five separate advisers who are not speaking to each other.

We manage the sequence.

People on the ground matter

International planning cannot be done properly from spreadsheets and government websites alone.

Rules tell you what is legally possible. Local experience tells you what is actually happening.

We work with professionals and contacts in the jurisdictions we use so that we understand how authorities, banks and institutions are treating particular situations in practice.

That allows us to identify problems earlier and avoid routes that may look possible in theory but are unlikely to work well in reality. It also helps us find opportunities that are easy to miss from outside the jurisdiction.

5. Ongoing support

The work does not necessarily finish once an application is approved.

Residencies have renewal requirements. Banking relationships are reviewed. Tax rules change. Businesses evolve. Families move.

A structure that was excellent three years ago may no longer be the best one today.

So where appropriate, we remain involved and review the position as circumstances change. The objective is not merely to establish something. It is to keep it working.

How we think

We are not interested in selling the same solution to every client.

We are interested in finding out how far the law genuinely allows us to go in pursuit of the client's objective, then building the cleanest, strongest and most discreet route within those boundaries.

Sometimes that means using the conventional answer. Sometimes it does not.

The standard

What matters is that the structure is legal, defensible and genuinely useful to the person it was built for.

Getting started

The first conversation is confidential and focused on the outcome.

We establish where you are now, where you want to get to and what stands between those two positions. From there, we determine whether there is a route worth pursuing — and, if there is, how best to build it.
